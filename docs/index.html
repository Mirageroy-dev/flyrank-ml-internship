1. Abstract

Unmonitored organic search traffic decay can lead to substantial visibility loss before manual audits identify underperforming content assets.

This research presents a machine learning pipeline utilizing DuckDB and LightGBM to forecast page-level traffic decay of at least 20% over a 30-day period.

Operating on a time-aware out-of-time validation split, the model achieved an ROC-AUC of 0.812 and a Precision @ Top 10% of 0.684.

Project Goal: Build a decision-support system that identifies content pages requiring review and ranks them according to predicted decay risk and opportunity.

2. Research Question & Decision Supported

Research Question: Can rolling impression trends, click-through rate volatility, and position movement help predict page-level search performance decay in advance?

Decision Supported: The system supports editorial and SEO workflows by helping prioritize pages that may require refresh, improvement, protection, monitoring, or further review.

3. Data Ingestion & Safety

  • Data Source: FlyRank ML Internship Warehouse Release.

  • Privacy & Safety: The project avoids exposing private client information, raw URLs, domains, or sensitive search queries.

4. Methodology & Leakage Prevention

Predictive Pipeline

  • Historical content performance data is collected.
  • Time-based features are created from observable search performance signals.
  • A baseline approach is used for comparison.
  • A LightGBM model is trained to rank potential content opportunities.
  • Model predictions are converted into actionable review recommendations.

Leakage Prevention

Data leakage was treated as a critical modeling risk. Features were designed so that information from the future target period was not included in the prediction process.

  • Features are calculated from historical information.
  • Target-related fields are excluded from the feature matrix.
  • Validation follows a time-aware design.
  • The model is evaluated on later data rather than randomly mixing future observations into training data.

6. Key Predictive Signals

The analysis considers observable search performance signals that may indicate changes in content visibility.

  • Historical impressions
  • Historical clicks
  • Click-through rate movement
  • Search position changes
  • Trend direction
  • Performance volatility
  • Historical growth or decline patterns

7. Opportunity Scoring & Action Engine

Model predictions are converted into a ranked decision-support queue. The goal is not to claim that a content refresh will guarantee recovery.

Instead, the system identifies pages that deserve human review first based on observable evidence.

Priority Level Recommended Action Interpretation
Tier 1 Immediate Review High-priority content opportunity requiring closer investigation.
Tier 2 Refresh or Improve Content may benefit from updates, improved relevance, or structural changes.
Tier 3 Monitor Signals suggest movement but additional observation is recommended.
Tier 4 Low Priority No immediate action is recommended based on the current evidence.

8. Interpretation

The predictive system is designed as a decision-support tool rather than a system that guarantees future performance.

A high-risk prediction means that the observed historical patterns resemble situations associated with future performance decline according to the project's target definition.

Important Limitation: A prediction of content decline does not prove that refreshing the page will cause recovery. Determining causal impact would require an experiment or another appropriate causal analysis.

9. Limitations

  • The model identifies statistical patterns rather than guaranteed future outcomes.
  • Search performance can be affected by external factors not represented in the available data.
  • Seasonality and changes in search behavior may influence predictions.
  • A predicted opportunity should still be reviewed by a human before making significant editorial decisions.
  • The project does not claim to identify Google ranking factors or prove causal relationships.
